05 Water-wise design

Plants that belong here. Water that goes where it should.

Native and adapted plants need less supplemental water once established. We pair them with smart irrigation so the garden looks settled in July, not just in April.

A few we plant often

Native or adapted to North Texas

  • Live oakQuercus fusiformisEvergreen shade tree, drought tolerant
  • Cedar elmUlmus crassifoliaShade tree that handles heavy clay
  • Texas redbudCercis canadensis var. texensisPink-purple flowers, March to April
  • Yaupon hollyIlex vomitoriaEvergreen shrub for sun or shade
  • Autumn sageSalvia greggiiBlooms March to November
  • Flame acanthusAnisacanthus quadrifidus var. wrightiiRed-orange flowers June to October; hummingbirds
  • Turk's capMalvaviscus arboreus var. drummondiiNative to Texas; red flowers in shade, May to November
  • Little bluestemSchizachyrium scopariumNative bunchgrass, copper-red in fall
  • Gulf muhlyMuhlenbergia capillarisPink plumes in October
  • Black-eyed SusanRudbeckia hirtaWildflower, blooms June to October

Smart irrigation

  • WaterSense-labeled smart controllers adjust watering to local weather and site conditions, which helps reduce overwatering.
  • Every new automatic system includes a rain sensor.
  • We program controllers to follow your city's current watering schedule.
  • North Texas clay swells when wet, shrinks when dry and takes on water slowly once it's soaked. Good grading, amended beds and the right plants keep water moving away from your house.
