What the yard wants, season by season

Fall September to November

  • Plant trees and shrubs. Texas A&M Forest Service puts planting season at November through early spring.
  • Sow bluebonnet and wildflower seed now for spring color.
  • Plant pansies, violas and ornamental kale for cool-season beds.
  • Skip the lawn fertilizer. Lawns are going dormant.

Winter December to February

  • Prune most trees and shrubs, late December through February (Texas A&M AgriLife).
  • Plant bare-root trees from January.
Rule of the season

Prune crape myrtles lightly. Never top them.

Spring March to May

  • Cut back autumn sage in early spring.
  • Plant warm-season color after the last frost.
  • In bloom: Texas redbud, Mexican plum and bluebonnets.
Rule of the season

Fertilize the lawn only after full green-up.

Summer June to August

  • Mow weekly.
  • Water deeply and infrequently, on your city's schedule.
  • In bloom: flame acanthus, black-eyed Susan and Turk's cap.

Water-wise design

Plants and irrigation that suit North Texas

  • Native and adapted plants need less supplemental water once established.
  • WaterSense-labeled smart controllers adjust watering to local weather and site conditions, which helps reduce overwatering.
  • Every new automatic system includes a rain sensor.
  • We program controllers to follow your city's current watering schedule. McKinney outdoor water use (opens in a new tab)
  • North Texas clay swells when wet, shrinks when dry and takes on water slowly once it's soaked. Good grading, amended beds and the right plants keep water moving away from your house.

The plant list Native or adapted to North Texas

  • Live oak Quercus fusiformis Evergreen shade tree. Drought tolerant.
  • Cedar elm Ulmus crassifolia Shade tree. Handles heavy clay.
  • Texas redbud Cercis canadensis var. texensis Small tree. Pink-purple flowers March to April.
  • Mexican plum Prunus mexicana Small tree. White flowers February to April.
  • Yaupon holly Ilex vomitoria Evergreen shrub. Sun or shade.
  • Texas sage Leucophyllum frutescens Evergreen shrub. Needs good drainage.
  • Autumn sage Salvia greggii Small shrub. Blooms March to November.
  • Flame acanthus Anisacanthus quadrifidus var. wrightii Shrub. Red-orange flowers June to October; hummingbirds.
  • Turk's cap Malvaviscus arboreus var. drummondii Native to Texas. Shade; red flowers May to November.
  • Texas lantana Lantana urticoides Perennial.
  • Little bluestem Schizachyrium scoparium Native bunchgrass. Turns copper-red in fall.
  • Lindheimer muhly Muhlenbergia lindheimeri Ornamental grass. Tolerates alkaline clay.
  • Gulf muhly Muhlenbergia capillaris Ornamental grass. Pink plumes in October.
  • Inland sea oats Chasmanthium latifolium Shade grass. Spreads.
  • Texas bluebonnet Lupinus texensis Wildflower. Sow in fall; blooms March to May.
  • Black-eyed Susan Rudbeckia hirta Wildflower. Blooms June to October.
